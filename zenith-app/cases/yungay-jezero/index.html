Why scientists chose Yungay

Yungay’s value is its chemistry as much as its dryness: published work reports only trace organic species, very low culturable bacteria, and non-biological breakdown of added organics in the soil.

NASA’s ARADS teams worked in the Atacama from 2016–2019, combining rover operations, drilling, sample handling, and life-detection instruments. NASA describes the project as a field test for future Mars exploration.

That makes Yungay a credible validation point for life-detection work—not a pure terrain twin of Jezero.

Where the engine’s cell adds—and where it breaks

Good for: screening terrain and bedrock conditions for rover/terrain testing. The best cell’s 2.0° slope and 186 m roughness are close to Jezero’s 2.1° and 192 m, and its mapped sandstone–mudstone is closer to a sedimentary delta setting than Yungay’s loose sediment.

Breaks for: life-detection site selection. The engine does not measure organic-carbon scarcity, oxidants, or soil chemistry. It cannot replace Yungay for instrument work that depends on those properties.

Literature check: the wider Pampa del Tamarugal region has been compared with Martian landforms, including inverted channels and evaporite-related terrain. The published studies do not identify this exact engine cell as their field site.

Validation story

The model independently recovers a respected analog near the top of its ranking. It then points to a northern cell with a stronger terrain-and-rock match for Jezero—a candidate for rover and terrain trials.

That is a useful extension of expert knowledge, not a claim that agencies overlooked an accessible site. Chile’s main highway is nearby; access is not offered as the reason for the difference.

How to read the comparison

Scores come from the supplied engine outputs. The cell coordinate is approximate at the grid scale. “Best cell” means the highest Jezero score across 30,000 cells in this run.

The temperature contrast remains large: both Chile sites are much warmer on their coldest night than Jezero’s 191 K.
